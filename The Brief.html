The Premise · §I

The people who promised to protect us
are busy, distracted, or not coming.

We are community tech leaders, neighbors, and small-business owners in Richmond. We started this series because we kept noticing the same thing: the people we’re told to rely on — service providers, platforms, agencies — keep leaving the room when we need them most. The grid flickers and they blame the weather. The breach happens and they send an apology email. The platform changes the rules and the small operator absorbs the cost.

So we’re building the alternative the old-fashioned way. In person, on Fridays, around a coffee. Practical skills first. Then mutual aid. Then a neighborhood that does not need permission to keep itself safe.

A Typology · §II

What we are preparing for, plainly stated.

Six conditions we are already living with, sized from quiet to acute. None are hypothetical.

PLATE I

The slow erosion.

The ad network that follows your phone into your bedroom. The data broker that resold your address last spring. Nothing breaks. Everything is watched. It is the climate of the modern internet, and we have stopped noticing the weather.

→ Chapter 01
PLATE II

The dropped connection.

The cell tower flakes for a county. The ISP’s upstream peers fight and your block goes dark for nine hours. The supports you assumed were permanent are vendor agreements, not infrastructure.

→ Chapter 02
PLATE III

The rumor in the group chat.

A neighbor forwards a generated image as if it is news. Local rumor moves faster than any local newsroom can verify. The bottleneck is not information. It is trust.

→ Chapter 05
PLATE IV

The supply pinch.

The service you paid for last year quietly retires. The update bricks the thermostat. The part takes nine weeks. Convenience and dependency are the same line item, read in different lights.

→ Chapter 03
PLATE V

The decision made elsewhere.

The account locks. The terms of service shift. The small operator wakes up deplatformed for reasons no person at the company can explain. Sovereignty is a setting, until it isn’t.

→ Chapter 01
PLATE VI

The neighborhood event.

The transformer fails. The water main breaks. The hurricane lands. FEMA arrives, eventually. The block survives or struggles on what it set up before the lights went out.

→ Chapter 04
The Curriculum · §III

Five chapters. One neighborhood.

A multi-year sequence, taught in person, in public, around coffee. Each chapter is free. Each chapter sends people home with the device or the skill needed to teach the next one.

01

Safe at the Table.

Ten Friday sessions on digital hygiene and small-network OPSEC. The grammar of staying private and connected when working in public. Every attendee leaves with the device used in class.

The Pledge · §V

Be the help. Help your neighbor become the help.

A short list of things the Cohort agrees to, freely and in public, on the morning the first chapter begins.

  1. I will show up, learn one thing, and teach it back to someone within a week.
  2. I will not pretend the platforms are coming to save us, and I will not panic about that.
  3. I will keep this work free, because dependency we charge for is dependency we’ve made.
  4. I will know two of my neighbors by name before the next outage.
  5. I will leave each session a little less reliant on a vendor than I was that morning.
